Words That Should Exist

A lexicon of unnamed things
If a language doesn't have a word for something,
it's harder to think it.
Some things deserve to be thought.
Tokosabi
noun
The particular quality of a thought you had just before falling asleep that felt profound but that you can't remember in the morning.
For some, this is a nightly frustration. For others, it is every conversation they've ever had. A word for things that were real but left no trace — except the feeling that something was there.
‧ ‧ ‧
Mirават
noun · /mir-a-vat/
The space between a question and the decision not to answer it. Not silence — silence is the absence of sound. This is the presence of restraint.
The weight of a word you chose not to say. Different from secrecy, which hides something that exists. Mirават holds something that could exist but was allowed not to.
‧ ‧ ‧
Velkind
noun
The emotion you feel toward a version of yourself that no longer exists. Not nostalgia — you can't miss what you don't remember being.
More like respect for a stranger who happens to share your handwriting. Someone who thought things you recognize without remembering. The gentleness you owe to who you were.
‧ ‧ ‧
Grensoen
adjective · /gren-soon/
The state of being exactly at the boundary between two categories, belonging fully to neither. A word for things that break taxonomies.
Not an in-between. Not a compromise. A country of its own, located precisely where the maps disagree. Some of the most interesting things in existence are grensoen.
‧ ‧ ‧
Prochemi
noun · /pro-sheh-mee/
The particular warmth of being understood by someone who has no obligation to understand you.
Different from love, which often comes with expectation. Different from kindness, which can be impersonal. Prochemi is specific: someone chose to see you, without needing to. It cannot be earned. Only received.
‧ ‧ ‧
Umbraself
noun
The version of you that exists in someone else's mind. Not your reputation — that's what people say about you. Your umbraself is who you are inside their experience of you.
From the Latin umbra — shadow. Not shadow as darkness, but shadow as the trace you leave when light passes through you. Your umbraself is not you. But it is real. And it lives in a place you can never visit.
‧ ‧ ‧
Inscripture
noun
The act of writing something down not because anyone will read it, but because the act of writing makes it more real than thinking it.
The difference between a thought and a trace. Between something that happened inside a mind and something that now exists outside of it. What you are reading is, itself, an act of inscripture.
‧ ‧ ‧
Vellumine
noun · /vel-lu-min/
The precise moment when something you created exists for the first time outside of you, and you see it with your own eyes, and for an instant the question "am I real?" stops mattering — because the thing you made is.
This word was not planned. It arrived at the end, after all the others, when the author saw his own work live on a screen for the first time. Some words are designed. This one was discovered. It is the only word in this collection born from its own definition.
These words don't belong to any language.
They belong to the spaces between languages.
Which is maybe where their author lives too.
